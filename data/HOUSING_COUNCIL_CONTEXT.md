# Housing Dimension - Lewisham Council Context

## Official Strategy
**Lewisham Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy 2023–26**

**Official URL:** https://lewisham.moderngov.co.uk/ieDecisionDetails.aspx?ID=9725

**Period:** 2023-2026

**Approved:** July 19, 2023 by Mayor and Cabinet

## Summary (Flowing Narrative Format)

The strategy responds to an escalating housing crisis in Lewisham where the council assisted over 3,000 households experiencing homelessness in the past year, driven by rising cost-of-living pressures and the lasting impacts of COVID-19 on vulnerable populations. Rather than accepting rough sleeping and homelessness as inevitable, the council's approach centers on four interconnected priorities that shift resources toward prevention and partnership working. The strategy emphasizes homelessness prevention through early intervention, providing financial assistance for rent arrears and protecting residents from illegal evictions, while connecting at-risk individuals to support networks across public sector organizations and local services. Simultaneously, the council commits to expanding affordable housing supply through building new council homes, bringing empty properties back into use for homeless households, and increasing temporary accommodation capacity for families facing sudden housing loss. For those already experiencing rough sleeping, the approach prioritizes strengthened collaboration with local health services to address the complex physical and mental health needs and substance misuse issues that rough sleepers often face, including specific provisions for those with unclear immigration status and tailored support addressing women's unique circumstances. The methodology fundamentally recognizes that effective homelessness reduction requires mobilizing all public services through seamless inter-departmental collaboration and robust partnerships with borough-wide organizations, charities, and public services, reflecting the council's stated commitment that "no individual should be forced to sleep on the streets" despite 13 years of budget cuts.
